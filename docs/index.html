01 — The project

Responsibility is
more than
taking the blame.

Keeping a promise. Accepting limits on power. Owning an unpopular decision. In politics, being responsible can mean very different things.

DEMOCRESP examines how politicians and citizens define, contest and evaluate political responsibility—and its opposite. It asks why conduct that some regard as responsible is rejected by others, and why apparently irresponsible behaviour can win public support.

At the heart of the project is a question of costs: do political actors accept the political, reputational and material costs of their choices, or shift them onto opponents, future generations and society?

The project connects elite discourse with citizens’ expectations to understand how agreement—and disagreement—about responsibility shapes trust, legitimacy and political behaviour.

02 — The conceptual framework

One contested idea.
Four dimensions.

The project starts with four dimensions of responsibility, while remaining open to the meanings citizens themselves bring to the concept.

01

Policy

What do we stand for?

Maintaining coherent policy commitments, even when doing so is electorally costly.

Explore the tension +

Taking responsibilityHolding to a policy commitment in the public interest.

Shifting the costsUndoing policies to retaliate against opponents rather than advance an alternative vision.

02

Institutional

Which limits do we accept?

Respecting checks, oversight and binding rules when they constrain partisan advantage.

Explore the tension +

Taking responsibilityAccepting constitutional checks and independent oversight.

Shifting the costsUndermining or bypassing constraints to secure a political advantage.

03

Moral

Who owns the decision?

Accepting liability, explaining choices and bearing the reputational costs of failure.

Explore the tension +

Taking responsibilityOffering explanations, sharing credit and accepting blame.

Shifting the costsScapegoating others and presenting contrition as weakness.

04

Temporal

Whose future is at stake?

Recognising past commitments and the long-term consequences of decisions made today.

Explore the tension +

Taking responsibilityAcknowledging inherited commitments and considering future generations.

Shifting the costsDisowning the past and passing today’s burdens on to successors.

These are objects of investigation: DEMOCRESP asks how elites and citizens interpret, combine and challenge them.

03 — The research programme

From political words
to democratic consequences.

Three connected work packages bring political communication, citizen perceptions and democratic citizenship into the same picture.

WP 01

Conceptual mapping
& elite strategy

How do political actors claim, contest and deny responsibility—and how do their strategies change across countries and over time?

Computational text analysisQualitative coding

Parliamentary debates, party manifestos and a new comparative corpus of leader and campaign speeches reveal how responsibility is framed in political competition.

Building the conceptual and empirical foundations
WP 02

Citizen perceptions

What does responsibility mean to citizens, and which claims resonate with whom, under which conditions?

Focus groupsPanel surveysExperiments

Citizens’ own definitions inform new measures and realistic survey experiments. A three-wave panel in Austria, Germany, Poland, Spain and the United Kingdom traces evaluations over time.

Connecting political claims with citizens’ expectations
WP 03

Democratic citizenship
& consequences

What happens when elites’ claims and citizens’ expectations align—or pull apart?

Comparative surveysElite interviews

The project studies trust, democratic legitimacy and electoral behaviour, and examines how political actors in Austria and the United Kingdom respond to accusations of irresponsibility.

Explaining consequences and the feedback to political elites

04 — People

A shared inquiry.

Based at the University of Innsbruck, DEMOCRESP brings together comparative politics, survey research and computational social science.

Principal investigator

Dr. Fabian Habersack

Department of Political Science
University of Innsbruck

Fabian studies political representation, party competition and populism. His research combines comparative data, survey experiments and computational text analysis to understand the relationship between political elites and citizens.
